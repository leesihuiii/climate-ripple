    Method & sources

    A teaching model: real research rounded into simple rules. Treat results as a sense of scale.

    • Footprint: car 0.17 kg CO₂e per km (petrol/diesel), 0.11 (hybrid), EVs at 0.18 kWh/km times your grid; public transport 0.06 kg per passenger-km; a short return flight 0.3 t and a long-haul return 1.8 t including contrail effects; grids at 0.75 / 0.40 / 0.05 kg per kWh. Based on UK government conversion factors.
    • Diets: 2.6 t (high meat) to 1.1 t (vegan) a year, from Scarborough et al. 2014.
    • Targets: 2.5 t per person by 2030 to stay on a 1.5 °C path, from the 1.5-Degree Lifestyles report. World average of about 7 t from 57 Gt total emissions in the UNEP Emissions Gap Report 2024.
    • Warming: 1.3 °C today plus 0.2 °C for every tonne per person, held until 2100. This is calibrated so today's average (about 7 t) gives roughly 2.7 °C, in line with current-policy projections.
    • Impacts: heat, rain and drought frequencies and sea level from the IPCC AR6 WGI; species and coral risk from IPCC AR6 WGII; crop yields from Zhao et al. 2017; people outside the human climate niche from Lenton et al. 2023. Values between published warming levels are interpolated.
    • Ice: glacier mass loss by 2100 (26% at 1.5 °C to 41% at 4 °C) from Rounce et al. 2023; ice-free Arctic Septembers from the IPCC (about once a century at 1.5 °C, once a decade at 2 °C). The year-by-year view eases warming and sea level from today to 2100; the drawings are not to scale.
    • Chain diagram: the splits between gases and impacts are simplified shares for illustration. Real impacts depend on total warming, not on any one person's tonnes.
    • Warming stripes design by Ed Hawkins, showyourstripes.info.
